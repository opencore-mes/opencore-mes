An object is a kind of record the plant keeps: its fields, the states it goes through, who may do what with it, the rules that check it, and how its form and list look. Each tab below sets one part. On a live design, or a change that is not open for your edits, every tab is read only.

Around the tabs

The tabs
Copilot, Changes, General, Fields, States, Roles & policies, Rules, Layout, Preview, Stewards, a tab per installed suite, then JSON. Click one to open it in this window.
The number on a tab
How many things on that tab differ from the published version. On Changes, it counts them all.
new, changed, removed
Marks inside a tab on a field, a policy or a form card that this change adds, changes or removes. Something removed stays listed, struck through, until the change executes.
Close this window
Shown when the change is split into several windows: closes this one. Nothing is lost; the others show the same draft.

Chips and conditions, more closely

Typing into a list of words
↑ ↓ move through the suggestions, Enter adds, Esc closes them. Where new words are allowed (a role, a state, an enum value), leaving the box adds what you typed; where only this object's fields or states fit, you pick from the list.
Building a condition
Each row is a thing to test (a field, the state, the person), how, and a value, or a field to compare with another. + condition adds a row, + group a group (all of, any of, none of these), + from a pattern… starts from a common one; × removes a row or a group. Done closes it. Problems are said in red under the box.
Its JSON
The same condition as text, applied as soon as it reads correctly. Empty means none.
Try
Type a value for each thing it reads, and see whether it holds. Nothing is saved.

Copilot

The conversation
Ask the AI to change the object (“add a moisture field Quality can edit while on hold”) or to explain it. Each design tool it used is listed with a tick or a cross. It works as you, saves into this draft, and never submits.
Not set up
Shown when no AI is configured for the plant, saying what is missing.
Ask the copilot…
Type your request. ⌘Enter or Ctrl+Enter sends it, like Send. What you type is kept if you leave the tab.
Attach
Adds up to five files for it to read: a picture, a PDF, a CSV file or an Excel workbook. You can also paste or drop one into the box.
Send
Saves your unsaved edits first, so it works on what you see, then sends. While it works, Working… shows which step it is on.
New conversation
Deletes the conversation kept with this change, after asking. The draft stays as it is.

Changes

What differs
Every difference from the published version, counted as changed, added and removed. Reviewers and approvers open the change here: it is what they pass or approve.
By tab
Differences are grouped by the tab they are on. Open … goes to that tab.
A difference
Click it to fold it open or closed. Lines with − are taken out, lines with + are put in. A group of four or fewer opens by itself.

General

Name
The object's name: lower case letters, digits and _. You can change it until its first version is approved; after that its records carry it, and only the label changes.
Label
What people see it called.
Area
The group it is listed under in the navigator.
Description
What it is for, in a sentence or two.
Title field
Pick the field that names a record in lists, tabs and search.
Analytics dimensions
Up to five of its fields, picked from the list. Each time a record enters a state their values are copied, so reports can group by the value a record had then.
Excel import and export
Tick what a file may do: be exported, create new records, update existing ones. Rows matched by picks the field that finds the record a row updates (or its record id). Imports go through the same policies and rules as the forms.
Flows
Tick how its records may take part in flow templates: as a traveler (goes along a route, like a lot), a resource (where a route's work is done), a subject (its events may set a plan off) or a reference (a flow may read it). None ticked: they take no part.
Its step field
For a traveler: the field holding the step it is at. A route writes it on entering a step, and writing it any other way moves the route there.

Fields

Built in, locked
Says when the platform or an installed suite relies on this object or some of its fields, and why. A locked field is kept and its type fixed; a lock beside its name says by whom.
Name
Click a name (with its pencil) to rename it: the form, list, policies, conditions and analytics follow, and the checks name scripts, transactions or screens that still read the old name. Once published, a name is fixed: change the label instead.
Label
What people see beside the field.
Type
Pick string, text, integer, decimal, boolean, date, enum (a list of values), ref (another object's record) or image.
Required
Ticked: a record cannot be saved without it. For “required only when…”, use Required when on the Layout tab.
Sensitive
Health or personal data: hidden wherever records are shown or exported, and never in queries, analytics or the AI. Someone who may read it sees it on the record after saying why, and each showing is recorded. Not for a reference or a picture.
Erasable
Personal data a privacy officer may erase from a record when someone asks: the value is replaced, the record and its history kept. Not for a picture.
Derived from a reference
Derive from a reference… makes a field the platform keeps from what the record's references point at: pick one of this object's references, then a field of what it points to (a lot's Product → Control), and on through another reference when that field is one. Use an expression turns it into a condition over the record and those fields (is its product military?). Nobody types it: it is worked out at every save, and again at once when the record it reads changes; the change that adds it fills every record already there. Never required, sensitive or erasable; the × makes it an ordinary field again.
Values
For an enum: its values, as chips (any new one may be added). Tick several values for a list of them; stored values are converted when the change executes.
What a file takes
For a file field (a work instruction, a drawing, a video of the job): tick picture, pdf, video or spreadsheet; none ticked takes pictures, PDFs and videos. A file is read through its record, by whoever may read that record; derived through a reference, a lot shows its step's.
Refers to
For a ref: pick the object its records point at.
Remove
Takes the field out, after asking. A locked field says kept instead.
New field name, Add
Type a name (lower case letters, digits and _), then Add. It starts as a string, placed at the end of the form's last section; set its type and label in its row.

States

A list
With one state and no transitions, its records have no lifecycle and show no state, like departments. List states to give it one.
States
Its states in order, as chips. Empty keeps it (or makes it) a list. Renaming a state records are in needs a migration.
Initial state
Pick the state a new record starts in.
Tones
For each state, pick how it shows: plain, neutral, info, ok, warn or danger. The badge beside it is how it will look; the plant's theme sets the colours.
Transitions
The object's actions, one per row. The action's name is fixed; type its Label (the button people see), pick the states it may start From (only this object's states), and pick the state it goes To.
Remove
Takes the transition out, after asking.
New action, Add
Type an action's name and Add: it goes from the initial state to the last one until you change it. Who may take it is set on Roles & policies.

Roles & policies

Nothing is allowed until a policy grants it. A policy grants reading, writing and actions to its roles while its condition holds.

Reserved to a certification
Before any policy: records only those holding a certification may see, whatever their roles, even if they read every record. Reserve records to a certification adds one: the certification (People & departments lists them) and For the records where, a condition on the record (empty: every record). A lot whose Military is derived from its product requires ITAR when it is military. Someone without it finds nothing of such a record anywhere, and a save that would make a record theirs no more is refused. Approved by the stewards and governance.
Roles
The roles this object declares; users and groups are assigned to them. Suggestions are the roles other objects use, to keep the plant's words alike; a new one is lower case letters, digits and _.
Overview
What each role may do, over every policy: read, create and archive records, each field, each action. Click its title to fold it. Colours: write, yes or allow; read; — not granted; denied (locked, hidden, refused). A * means only under a condition.
A role's name
Click it to show only the policies that name that role; click again, or Show all, for every policy.
A cell
Click it to see, under the grid, which policies make it so. Double-click it (or press Enter) to change it at once, when one policy is that role's alone and always applies.
Which policies make it so
Each policy's grant as a tile: click it for the next level. It says when a policy is shared with other roles, or holds only under its condition, and when a deny wins. Grant it in a new policy for … alone adds a policy for that role only; close hides this.
A policy
Its name, a mark if this change adds or changes it, rename, and remove (after asking). The name only says what the policy is for: what it grants is what is ticked below it.
In short
Under each policy's settings, the policy in one sentence as it stands: who may read, create, archive, which fields they read or write, which actions they take, its condition in words, the transactions it is limited to and the fields it never lets them write. It follows every tick.
With its other policies
For each of its roles that other policies also name: what that role may do once they are all counted (desktop-read and desktop-edit together), noting when some of it holds only under a condition.
What to look at
Said in the same place when it matters: roles that may change records but read them through no policy (they could not open one), and a name that says the opposite of what it grants (named edit, changing nothing; named read, changing records).
Set it to
Read only (records and every field, read), Read and edit (read and create records, write every field) or Nothing: sets what is ticked at once, to change from there. Its roles, condition, transactions and denied fields stay as they are.
Its roles
Whom it is for. A new role typed here is declared on the object too.
Only when
A condition on the record and the person; none means always. Its roles still decide whom it is for.
Only through transactions
Transactions by name, such as move_in: the policy then grants only to writes made through them. Empty: any write.
may read, create, archive and restore records
Tick what it allows with whole records.
Fields
A tile per field: click it to go from — to read to write and round again. * every other field is every field the policy does not name.
Actions allowed
A tile per transition: click it to allow it, or take it back.
Deny writing these fields
Fields its roles may never write while it holds, whatever another policy grants. Only this object's fields.
New policy id, Add
Type an id and Add: the new policy is for the first role, and reads records and every field until you change it.

Rules

The rule pipe: scripts that check and complete a record, in order, on every change of state. Each takes the record's context and returns it, or refuses with a message.

The pipe
One row per script, in the order they run. edited marks a script this change alters.
A script's name
Click it to open the script below.
May write
The fields the script may set. Changing any other stops the pipe, so a reviewer sees from here what each script can alter.
Backend only
Ticked: it runs on the server only, as for a script that looks up other records.
After commit
Ticked: it runs after the record is saved, not before.
Earlier, Remove
The arrow moves a script one place up the pipe. Remove takes it out of this object's pipe, after asking; the script itself stays.
Add a published script, Add to pipe
Pick a script that already exists, then Add to pipe: it runs last.
New script name, Add
Type a name and Add: a new script, from a short template, joins the pipe and opens below.
The script
Its code. A published script is shown as it is live until you edit it; then this change holds its new version. Under it, whether it compiles, or the first problem.
Dry run
Runs the draft on the server, as you, with the input in its box, and shows what it gave and would write: nothing is saved. Run runs it; Reset input brings the example back.
Save as test case
After a run, name what it shows and save it: the fitness test runs it again on every submit.
Test cases
Each with its last result. A new or changed script needs at least one. JSON shows them as text; remove takes one out, after asking.

Layout

The list and the form people see for this object.

List columns
Its columns, in order. The arrows move one earlier or later, × removes it, + column adds a field.
Sorted by
Pick the field the list is sorted by, then ascending or descending; or last changed first.
Search first
For an object with too many records to browse: its list starts empty, with a search box. Development, demo and test instances list them anyway.
Form tabs
Click a tab to lay it out. + tab adds one.
A tab's label, move, Remove tab
With several tabs: type its label, move it left or right, or remove it. Its fields go to Not on the form.
A section
Type its label. Tick can fold to let people fold it, and folded at first to start it folded. The arrows move it up or down, the drop-down moves it to another tab, and Remove takes it out (its fields go to the tray).
A field's card
Drag it to move it, within a section or to another. Click it to set it up below. It shows how the field is drawn, its width of 12, and whether it has conditions.
Its buttons
The arrows move it earlier or later; − and + make it narrower or wider. Widths are of a 12-column row; on a narrow screen every field takes the whole row.
Add section
Adds an empty section to this tab. Drop fields into it.
Not on the form
Fields the form does not show. Drag one into a section, or pick place in…; drag a card here to take it off the form.
Width, Drawn as, Help text, Placeholder
For the selected field: how much of the row it takes, the control it is drawn as (such as a dropdown, radio list, buttons, toggle, search as you type, scan), and words shown under it or inside it while empty. A text field also has Rows.
Shown when, Enabled when, Required when
Conditions on the form as it is filled in, the record as saved, and the person. None: always shown and enabled, and required only as the Fields tab says. Required when is checked by the server too. They change only what the form shows; what a person may read or write is the policies'.
Take it off the form
Moves the selected field to the tray. It stays a field of the object.
Preview
The tab you are laying out, as people will see it, redrawn on every edit.

Preview

The form
The whole form as this draft lays it out, with its inputs disabled, redrawn on every edit in any window. Split opens it beside the tab you are editing.

Stewards

Stewards are the departments that approve changes to what they steward.

The object
Tick the departments that steward the whole object.
Fields, States
Tick departments for one field or one state. One with none ticked is stewarded by the object's.
Approval of record changes
Changes to its records made outside a transaction (on a form, in a list, by an import) can wait until these stewards approve them, never by the person who asked. A transaction never waits.
Changing values, a new record, actions
Tick which changes wait: changed values, a new record, or actions (when it has any).
Only these
Narrow what waits to some fields, some states or some actions. None ticked: any.

A tab per installed suite

The suite's tab
Each suite installed here may add a tab, named by the suite, before JSON, with its own part of the object's design. What you set there is part of the same draft, reviewed and approved with the rest.

JSON

The definition
The whole object as JSON, for integrators. Edit it here; nothing changes until you apply it.
Apply JSON
Replaces the draft with what you typed, and every check runs on it as on any other edit. The object's name must stay the same; if the text is not JSON, it says what is wrong.
